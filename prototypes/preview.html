LODVault Logo Prototypes

Eight directions for a flat, modern, and elegant redesign — exploring minimalism, negative space, color restraint, and typographic clarity.

Current Logo

Gradient Flag + White L

The existing logo uses a Luxembourg-flag vertical gradient inside a rounded square, with a white "L" formed by two rounded rectangles. The gradient adds depth but makes it harder to read at small sizes, and the shape feels more decorative than modern.

Prototype 01

Minimal Flat

Clean tricolor bands, solid white L

Replaces the gradient with three flat, solid horizontal bands — the authentic Luxembourg flag. The L is a crisp white silhouette. Eliminates the gradient's visual noise while keeping full national color identity.

"Flat color is honest; gradients pretend to be 3D."
Prototype 02

Monoline

Single stroke, infinite scalability

A single-weight stroke L on deep navy, with a thin tricolor bar at the base as a subtle national nod. Inspired by Linear, Notion, and Vercel — the absolute minimum mark. The stroke-only L reads cleanly at any size, even at 16px.

"The stroke is the atom — everything else is composition."
Prototype 03

Negative Space

The shape defines the void

A solid dark circle with the L carved out as white negative space. No rounded square, no tricolor — pure silhouette on a dark field. The circular container feels app-icon-native and the contrast is maximum for small-size legibility.

"What you remove matters more than what you add."
Prototype 04

Deconstructed

Red vertical, blue horizontal — the L split into two colors

Breaks the L into its two constituent strokes and assigns each a Luxembourg flag color: red for the vertical, blue for the horizontal. Light background keeps it airy. Bauhaus-inspired geometric honesty — each part is exactly what it is.

"Two bars, two colors, one letter."
Prototype 05

Rounded Capsule

Pill shapes, soft geometry

Each stroke of the L is a pill/capsule shape with fully rounded ends, sitting on a dark navy background. A small tri-dot cluster in the corner carries the flag colors without dominating. Soft, approachable, and unmistakably modern — the iOS/Safari aesthetic.

"Comfort in corners; confidence in curves."
Prototype 06

Tricolor Carve

L cuts across all three bands

Three bold horizontal flag bands form the field, and a single white L shape punches through all three. The L becomes a unifying shape that bridges the three colors — symbolic of LODVault bridging the dictionary and the user's personal study.

"A shape that unites stripes becomes a logo that unites meaning."
Prototype 07

Dot Accent

Near-monochrome, single pop of color

A white L on near-black, with a single blue dot in the bottom-right corner. Maximum restraint — the flag is reduced to a decorative seal. This is the "vault" metaphor: the heavy dark container with one luminous accent, like a lock on a safe.

"One color is enough when it's in the right place."
Prototype 08

Outline Flag

Stroke L + ribbon edge accent

A stroke-only L on dark navy, paired with a thin vertical tricolor ribbon along the left edge. The L has sharp square corners (miter joins) for a technical, editorial feel. The ribbon adds nationality without center stage — like a book spine or passport edge.

"The flag doesn't need to be the face — it can be the spine."
★ Merged

Deconstructed Dark

Split-color L on navy — 04's L, 05's stage

The two-color deconstructed L from prototype 04 (red vertical, blue horizontal) placed on the deep navy background from prototype 05, with its tricolor dot cluster as a subtle accent. The dark field lets the split colors glow — each bar becomes a luminous stroke. Best of both: geometric honesty from the deconstruction, depth and focus from the dark container.

"Two colors on dark don't fight — they shine."

💡 Recommendation

Prototype 09 (Deconstructed Dark) is the strongest merged candidate — it combines the geometric clarity and color symbolism of the split L (04) with the focus and depth of the dark container (05). On the navy background, the red and blue bars gain contrast and luminosity that the light background couldn't provide. The tricolor dot cluster adds a finishing touch without competing with the main mark.

Among the original eight, Prototypes 02 (Monoline) and 07 (Dot Accent) remain the strongest ultra-minimal directions — both scale flawlessly to 16px. Prototype 03 (Negative Space) is the boldest departure for a premium, globally neutral feel.

If you want national identity front and center with modern flat execution, 09 gives you the best balance: the two Luxembourg colors are intrinsic to the mark itself, not decorative — they are the mark.

White pill L, 14px ribbon

10c — Pill + Wide Ribbon

White pill L, 24px ribbon

The ribbon becomes a structural element — a colored spine anchoring the composition. At this width the tricolor reads immediately even at small sizes. The pill L floats freely beside it.

10d — Colored Pill + Thin Ribbon

Now the L itself carries flag colors — red vertical, blue horizontal — just like 04 and 09. The thin ribbon adds a white stripe to complete the tricolor. Maximum flag presence inside the mark.

White pill L, 14px ribbon, dot cluster accent

Full reunion: the pill L from 05, the ribbon from 08, and the dot cluster from 05 — all three accents in one composition. The ribbon anchors the nationality; the dots add rhythm and lightness. Maximum warmth.

🔬 10e Refinements

Three specific hypotheses tested against the 10e baseline — which changes actually improve it?
10e — Baseline
→
Three potential issues spotted: ① the dots float disconnected in the top-right, ② the white ribbon stripe competes with the white L, ③ the equal-weight bars of the L feel heavy — typography favors lighter horizontals. Below: targeted tests of each.

11a — Dots to Bottom-Right

Hypothesis ①: anchor the orphaned dots

Moves the tricolor dot cluster from top-right to bottom-right. The dots now feel like a seal or signature beneath the L, grounded against the navy field rather than floating. The top-right corner becomes clean negative space.

Only change: dots moved from (408, 120–160) → (408, 380–420)

11b — Red-Blue Ribbon Only

Hypothesis ②: remove the redundant white stripe

The L is already white, so the white stripe in the ribbon duplicates information. This replaces the three-stripe ribbon with a two-stripe red-blue ribbon. More distinctive visually — the flag is now uniquely represented, not loosely echoed.

Only change: ribbon from red/white/blue → red/blue (split 50/50)

11c — Thinner Horizontal Bar

Hypothesis ③: typographic refinement

The horizontal bar reduced from 64px to 52px height (rx adjusted to 26). In fine typography, horizontals are lighter than verticals — this creates a more elegant, less blocky L. The vertical carries weight; the horizontal provides direction.

Only change: horizontal bar 64→52px, radius 32→26

11d — All Three Combined

★ Best candidate if all three hypotheses hold

Applies all three refinements at once: bottom-right dots at full opacity, red-blue-only ribbon, and thinner horizontal bar. If each change improved 10e individually, this should be the strongest version. Compare carefully against the 10e baseline above.

Changes: dots→bottom, full opacity; ribbon→red/blue only; horizontal→52px
